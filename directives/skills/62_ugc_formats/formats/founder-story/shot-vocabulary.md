# Shot vocabulary, Founder Story

Measured from the ten banked founder story ads in this skill's own bank, 2026-08: 189 shots,
18.9 shots per ad. The HIGHEST shot count in the family.

This file has two halves.

**The PROMPT BLOCK below is the only part that reaches the video model.** It is pasted verbatim
into the render prompt. A conditional shot row may be DROPPED when the product cannot support
it; nothing is ever added, renamed, or given a different number.

**Everything under THE MEASUREMENTS is engine-facing** and is never pasted: provenance, the raw
counts the prompt block was derived from, doctrine notes, and the reasoning behind house rules
that override the bank. It exists so the numbers can be re-checked, not so a model can read them.

---

## PROMPT BLOCK

<!-- PROMPT-BLOCK-START -->
FORMAT: FOUNDER STORY UGC

This kind of ad is simple. The person who made the thing tells you why, while you watch clips
from their actual life and their actual business.

The rules:

1. Their voice runs over the pictures. They appear on camera only in short bursts.
2. Every shot is short. Never hold one.
3. Open on a real moment from the story, not on someone introducing themselves.
4. Show the real work: boxes, tape, warehouses, kitchens, hands making things.
5. Let their clothes change through the ad. The clips come from different days.
6. Move through time: how it started, the rough early version, what it is now.
7. Never let it sound like a brand talking.

THE HOOK
Shot 1, 1 to 2 seconds. Make it stop the scroll. A lot should happen in it.

THE SHOTS THIS FORMAT USUALLY USES
Close on hands and objects. The founder somewhere real. Wide on the place itself. Very close on
materials. The founder talking straight to camera. Use them however you want. Two things hold:
nothing is ever held for long and this ad carries more shots than any other kind; and it is the
one format that uses wide shots, because the story happened in real places.

The founder talking is filmed still. Everything else drifts, like a phone held while working.

HOW THEY PLAY IT
Telling you something that actually happened to him, and it still means something. Unpolished
and direct, occasionally amused at his younger self. Never a brand line.

TIMING
At least two shots run under half a second and at least one runs over three seconds. Fast and
steady rather than spiky: many short shots, none long. Every shot change is a hard cut or a jump cut.
<!-- PROMPT-BLOCK-END -->

## THE MEASUREMENTS, engine-facing, never pasted


## THIS IS A NARRATED MONTAGE ACROSS TIME AND PLACES

```
18.9 shots per ad         nearly three times the expert format
longest banked shot       5.6 seconds     nothing is ever held
voiceover 137  :  on-camera 32
```

Four out of five spoken lines are VOICEOVER laid over pictures. The founder narrates while the
ad shows their life and their business. They appear on camera, but in short bursts scattered
through the montage rather than as the spine of it.

**Every shot is short.** The longest shot in the entire bank is 5.6 seconds, and banked averages
run between 1.0 and 2.7 seconds. This is the only format in the family where nothing is allowed
to hold.

## WHO IS ON SCREEN

**The founder, in many different clothes.** The bank describes outfits CHANGING across a single
ad, because the clips come from different days and different years: "outfits change between
black T-shirt/light jeans/boots, black crop top, pale-pink lounge set, dark studio looks,
oversized blazer." Wardrobe change is how the format says time passed.

**Other people appear briefly and unnamed.** A colleague, a partner, a child, a team user. One
banked ad uses a seated INTERVIEWER asking the founder questions, which is a legitimate variant.

## WHAT IS ALWAYS IN FRAME, ACROSS THE AD

Real business and life texture, not staged product photography:

- shipping cartons, mailers, packing tape, stock pallets, fulfillment tables
- workrooms, warehouses, studios, kitchens, labs, offices, retail shelves
- hands making, packing, labelling, testing, measuring
- ordinary domestic places where the story actually started
- the product at every stage of its existence, from raw ingredient to finished box

## HOW THE CAMERA BEHAVES

**Mixed, and the mixture is the format.** The bank is consistent: "interview shots static, B-roll
mostly handheld or drifting", "mostly static, slight handheld drift in delivery footage",
"handheld first-person walking/entry footage leads into a softly lit office."

So the founder talking is filmed still. Everything else drifts, walks, and moves like someone
holding a phone while doing something else.

**The footage is low grade and that is correct.** The banked resolutions are 360x640, 540x960 and
576x1024, the lowest in the family, described as archive phone clips. A founder story is
assembled out of footage that already existed, not shot in an afternoon.

## THE HOOK, shot one

```
duration, banked:   median 1.25s, range 0.4 to 4.5   house rule: 1 to 2 seconds
under 2 seconds:    6 of 10   <-- this format already opens fast on its own
```

The banked hooks are the most varied in the family and none is a person introducing themselves:
a woman reclining in a hospital bed holding a sachet, a hand gripping car keys, tattooed hands
pulling a can tab, an oven interior with a roast in it, hands lifting the top cookie off a
stack, a mother lying with a newborn on her chest.

**The hook is a MOMENT from the story, not the beginning of it.** Drop the viewer into a specific
physical scene, then let the voiceover explain what they are looking at.

**The first words start the sentence the picture cannot finish.** "Instant Hydration was born out
of desperation." "Two years ago I launched a..." "I'm a 26 year old..."

## THE SHOT TYPES

This is the only format that uses WIDE shots. The framing range is the whole vocabulary of film,
because the story spans places and years.

| Shot type | Required | Share | Duration | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CLOSE-UP, hands, objects, details | always | ~15% | 0.2 to 3s | voiceover |
| MEDIUM, the founder in a place | always | ~12% | short | either |
| WIDE, the place itself | always | ~11% | short | voiceover |
| MACRO, ingredients, materials, product detail | always | ~5% | very short | voiceover |
| FOUNDER TO CAMERA | always | ~8% | short | on camera |
| PROFILE or FULL BODY | optional | ~5% | short | voiceover |

**WIDE.** The workshop, the shop floor, the street, the kitchen. Establishes that this happened
somewhere real. Rare in every other UGC format and normal here.

A conditional row is DROPPED when the story does not contain it. No row is added or renamed.

## WHAT USUALLY HAPPENS

A specific moment opens it, then the voice takes over and the pictures run ahead of the words.
The ad moves between periods: the problem that started it, the early makeshift version, the
work of building, the product as it exists now. Locations change constantly and so do the
clothes. Hands are shown doing real work. The founder appears in short direct bursts to say the
things only they can say. The product accumulates meaning by being shown at every stage rather
than by being demonstrated.

---

## SOUND

The founder's voice over almost everything, with brief on-camera bursts. Underneath it, the real
sound of wherever each clip is: a workshop, a street, a kitchen, a room. Because the clips come
from different places, the ambience changes with them. No music anywhere.

## RHYTHM

```
banked CV of shot lengths:   0.59          shortest 0.20s, longest 5.60s
```

**Stated as counts in the prompt, because "make it uneven" does not land.** A live run
asked for uneven shots in prose and returned every shot within a second of the mean. Say this
instead:

```
At least two shots run under 0.5 seconds and at least one runs over 3.0 seconds.
```

Fast and even-tempered rather than fast and spiky: many short shots, none of them long. A
nineteen-shot founder story in thirty seconds averages 1.6 seconds a shot and the variation sits
around that, not across a ten-second spread. This is the one format where holding a shot is the
error.

## NEVER RENDERED

**Transitions are post-production, not generation.** No dissolves, fades, wipes, whip pans, zoom
transitions or speed ramps. Every shot change is a hard cut or a jump cut, and a montage is
especially prone to attracting cross-dissolves that must not be asked for.

## DELIVERY LINE, pasted into the render prompt

This is the last line of the video engine's V.4a pacing block. It replaces the generic one,
because this format does not offer a free choice:

```
You decide which lines the founder speaks on camera and which are carried as voiceover over the montage.
```
