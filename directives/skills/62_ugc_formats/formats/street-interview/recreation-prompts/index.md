# Recreation prompt bank, Street Interview

Ten PAIRS. Every pair is one video recreation prompt plus the anchor frame recreation
prompt that goes with it. They are STUDY MATERIAL, not templates.

```
video/   <key>_recreation_prompt.txt          the timed shot-by-shot video prompt
anchor/  <key>_anchor_recreation_prompt.txt   the still that locks identity for it
```

## How the two halves work together

The anchor prompt builds ONE still. That still is what every scene of the video attaches
as its identity reference, so the character and the product stay the same person and the
same object from the first frame to the CTA. Write the anchor first, get it approved,
then render video off it. A video generated without an approved anchor drifts.

The anchor prompts are written for product SUBSTITUTION: the user's uploaded product
image is the only product reference, it replaces the source product completely, and every
printed word, proportion, finish and logo on it survives unchanged while the composition,
pose, framing and lighting around it hold.

## Three rules bind every use of this bank

1. Read and analyze ALL TEN PAIRS before writing a single prompt for this format. The
   point is the pattern across ten, not any one of them.
2. NEVER copy one. Not a scene, not a sentence, not a shot list. They are reference and
   inspiration for how a prompt of this format is STRUCTURED and how specific it has to be.
3. They were built by auditing every frame and the audio of a real ad, so they carry small
   errors. Treat a detail that looks wrong as a transcription slip, never as a rule to copy.

What to take from them: the anatomy on both sides, the ALTITUDE of the detail, the way
camera, prop and lighting facts are stated as physical facts rather than as mood, and the
length each half lands at.
