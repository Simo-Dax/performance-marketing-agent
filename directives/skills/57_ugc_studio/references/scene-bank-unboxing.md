# Scene Bank — UNBOXING / FIRST IMPRESSION (UGC Studio format 03), full teardown of two winning ads

Two member-supplied winning ads, torn down word-for-word and frame-by-frame on 2026-07-10
(faster-whisper word timestamps + frame grids, the local method). Third seed bank for the
UGC Studio skill, covering BOTH faces of the format: Ad X is the FULL UNBOXING (a shipped
box physically opened on camera, contents out, used, eaten); Ad Y is the FIRST-IMPRESSION
REVEAL (the supplement/beauty equivalent — a drop-catch product reveal, label inspect,
contents poured out). Same fill rules as the other banks: shells recreate the source scene
shapes; fills swap product, category, packaging, and settings; one shell serves at most ONE
scene per ad; record `source_shell` (X01…, Y01…, or "synthesized").

Every shell below carries its render-audit verdict (2026-07-10): **GREEN** renders as
written; **YELLOW** renders with the named adaptation; **RED** never renders as written —
the replacement route is named inline. The laws the verdicts cite, the edit-grammar
mechanics, and the stitch QC all live in `render-laws.md`; the voice-cut and parallel
submission rules live in `voice-and-parallel.md`; the manifest/EDL contract lives in
`pipeline-contracts.md`. This bank defers to all three.

THE DURATION LAW: every generation is an integer 4–9 seconds. THE CAPTION LAW: Ad X burns
phrase-synced captions and Ad Y burns one static announcement caption — both are source-only
design layers; our renders ship CLEAN (the VO carries the words; `captions.txt` sidecar for
the member's editor). THE CLAIMS LAW: the sources' brands, offers, and claims ("Factor",
"chef-prepared", "tap here and use my code", "Goli", "MEGA-SALE on 5 months supply") are
legal-risk slots — fills use the member's sanctioned claims and real offer only. THE
PRODUCT LAW: every package, label, and tray renders from the member's REAL product photos
attached; label text is never invented.

RATIO NOTE: Ad X is 4:5 (feed crop); Ad Y is 9:16. Fills render 9:16 per the pipeline; the
member crops 4:5 in the editor if they want the feed frame.

## AD X — "Factor lifesaver" (full unboxing → usage → CTA, narrated)

Creator: woman in a pink fleece, bright white kitchen, marble counter. The branded shipping
box is the co-star: on the counter in frame one, opened with a cutter, emptied layer by
layer, and still in frame during the stack beat. First-person VO narrates over the whole
arc (no lip-sync except the final CTA beat). Burned phrase captions bottom-center
(source-only).

### The voiceover (verbatim, whisper-timed)

"Factor has been such a lifesaver for me. They deliver fresh, chef-prepared meals straight
to your door every week, and all I have to do is heat them up in the microwave or oven. I
love that there are so many options to choose from based on what I'm in the mood for and
everything is ready in minutes, which makes lunch or dinner so easy on hectic days. If
you're looking for something quick, convenient, and actually delicious, you have to try
Factor. Tap here to check it out and use my code to save."

### The systems this ad banks

- **The verdict-first open.** The FIRST spoken line is the conclusion ("such a lifesaver"),
  delivered over the tape-cut. The unboxing that follows is evidence for a verdict already
  on the table — the inverse of suspense, and it front-loads the brand in second one.
- **The tape-cut hook.** Frame one: branded box + blade through tape. The single most
  literal "something is about to be revealed" action that exists; brand legible before a
  word lands.
- **The packaging ritual.** Insulation and ice pack get their own beat. Touching every
  layer reads as honest delivery proof (it arrived REAL and cold), and it stretches the
  open-loop one beat longer.
- **Item-out → variety flip → stack.** Three escalating contents beats: the first item
  (what it is), the label flip-through (how many kinds), the counter stack (how much). The
  stack shot beside the emptied box is the value-for-money frame.
- **Usage completes the loop.** The unboxing does not end at the box: sleeve off, into the
  oven, eaten at the counter. Box → mouth in one arc is what separates an unboxing AD from
  unboxing content.
- **The eating CTA.** The ask lands while she is mid-meal, gesturing at the camera —
  the product is literally in use as the code is offered. Offer + code close (member's
  real offer only).
- **Hands carry it.** ~80% of the runtime is hands + box + trays; the face appears only to
  cook and eat. Low identity load: face image optional for most generations.

### The systems this ad banks

- **The drop-catch hook.** The product falls INTO the frame — gravity as the pattern
  interrupt, the catch as the resolution. One second, no words, brand legible on landing.
- **The label orbit.** A slow single-hand rotation makes the REAL label the whole story:
  name, mechanism, badge icons. Only legal with the real product photo attached — label
  text is never invented.
- **Contents-out display.** The bottle next to a bowl of its own contents is the
  supplement/beauty equivalent of an emptied box: what's inside, made visible and
  abundant. Sunlight does the appetite work.
- **The announcement frame.** The (source) caption is a NEWS + OFFER line — "just
  released… MEGA-SALE…". At fill this converts to a short VO spine or lives in the ad
  copy: news verb + product + mechanism + the member's real offer.
- **Totally faceless, two generations.** Hands only, two locations, ~11.6 seconds. The
  cheapest format variant banked so far.

## Cross-ad laws for the UNBOXING / FIRST IMPRESSION format (what a fill must keep)

1. **The package/product is on screen in frame ONE, brand legible,** and the opening
   action starts immediately: a blade through tape (X) or the drop-catch (Y). No settling
   in, no empty-handed intro.
2. **Two legal opens:** VERDICT-FIRST (the conclusion spoken over the opening action — the
   banked winner) or SUSPENSE ("let's see if it's worth the hype"). Pick one; never both.
3. **The reveal escalates in three stages:** the container → the layers/first item → the
   full contents (the stack / the bowl). Each stage gets its own beat; never jump from
   sealed box to full spread in one cut.
4. **Real packaging only.** Every box panel, label, sleeve, and tray renders from the
   member's real product/packaging photos attached to the generation; label and box text
   is never invented (the product law + screen-law cousin). Fine-print faces never render
   at all — they are still-inserts from the real photos (see the X05/X07 verdicts).
5. **An unboxing AD completes the loop into USAGE** — cook it, wear it, taste it, apply
   it (X08–X09). Reveal-only fills (Y) are legal for announcement/offer framings, where
   the display loop is the close.
6. **Hands carry the format.** Face optional (X shows it only to cook/eat; Y never does) —
   most generations attach body + product only, prompts keep the face out of frame; the
   full-arc variant may end on-camera for the CTA.
7. **The value frame:** show the QUANTITY once — the counter stack or the overflowing
   bowl. Abundance is this format's proof beat.
8. **Two legal closes:** the eating/using CTA with the member's real offer + code (X), or
   the held display loop with the offer in ad copy (Y). No end cards.
9. **Pace:** narrated fills at ~3.7 wps (measured); reveal fills are silent or a 3-line VO
   spine. Full arc 22–30s (~4 gens + still flashes, 15-shot timeline density); reveal
   10–14s (2 gens, 2 shots).
10. **Ships clean.** Source captions (phrase-synced or static announcement) are editor-side
    layers — our renders never burn text; `captions.txt` sidecar on request.

## Per-format intake — what the studio collects before an unboxing order renders

Enforced at intake (Step 1) for every ordered unboxing/reveal ad. The standing rule
governs all of it: **no photo → that beat becomes a still-insert from what exists, or is
cut. Never invented.**

- **Product photo(s)** — one product reference per generation, always.
- **EVERY printed packaging face, photographed:** box top, box sides, the message panel,
  the printed inner lid/inner packaging. A printed face with no photo never appears
  legible on screen.
- **Every shown variant's OWN photo** — the variety flip needs one real photo per
  flavor/variant; a variant without a photo is cut from the flip.
- **Open-state and contents-out photos:** the open box interior, the unsleeved /
  cooked / plated state, the contents displayed (the overflowing bowl). These are the
  real-photo states the manipulation ladder's top rungs (tape-cut, open/close, pour,
  bite/eat) require.
- **Product dimensions** — real sizes stated inline in every prompt that shows the product.
- **Face + body refs.** Face is needed ONLY for the eating-CTA beat in the Ad X shape
  (the identity checkpoint gen). The Ad Y shape is hands-only: per the identity-checkpoint
  rule for hands-only formats, either add one face-visible render or knowingly downgrade
  to a hands checkpoint — the member picks, on the record.
- **Voice clip ≤15s** — required for narrated fills (Ad X shape); a purely silent
  Ad Y-shape order skips it. Cut/fingerprint rules per `voice-and-parallel.md`.
- **The real offer / discount code** — the eating CTA speaks it; the reveal shape carries
  it in the caption sidecar or ad copy. Sanctioned claims only.
