# Talking-Object Art Direction — a cute Pixar-style world where the CAST sells the ad

Think like two people at once: a **direct-response creative strategist** (does this sell?)
and a **senior 3D animation director** (does this look like a frame from a premium
animated commercial?). The talking-object format's one big difference from every other
factory in this plugin: **the characters SPEAK ON CAMERA.** Dialogue is not a bug here —
it is the format. Every design choice below serves a character who talks.

## Core material rule (absolute — the app's locked Talking Object style)

**EVERYTHING in frame is one cohesive cute Pixar-style 3D animated world.** No real human
being, photoreal person, or live-action element EVER appears in any frame. The locked
Studio style block (from the il sistema app, style id `talking-object` — copied verbatim
into every prompt):

> Cute Pixar style ultra detailed 3D animated render, smooth professional textures,
> glossy realistic product surface, shallow depth of field, expressive but believable
> posing, cinematic and high end, with a cohesive warm, vibrant color grade.

(When the scene contains NO physical product — a generic character like an enzyme in a
body world — drop the clause "glossy realistic product surface"; it pushes organic
characters toward plastic. Everything else stays verbatim.)

## The character design law (the app's Talking Object rule)

Every talking character is **the object ITSELF with a face built into its own body: big
expressive eyes, a visible animated mouth, and tiny arms** — never a mascot standing next
to the object, never a floating face. The object IS the subject:

- **A real product as a character** (competitor bottle, hero bottle): keep its **real
  shape, label and colors fully intact so it still reads as the real object** — the eyes
  and mouth are built INTO the label art, every word of the label stays legible. This is
  why personified-product scenes REQUIRE the real product still as a generation reference
  (the reference ladder, tier 2).
- **A generic/organic character** (an enzyme, an ingredient root, a leaf, a molecule, a
  problem personified): the body is made OF the thing itself — a gnarled ashwagandha root
  with root-tendril legs, a coleus leaf with an ember core, a limp muscle fiber with
  drooping eyes. Face built into the body, big expressive eyes, visible mouth, tiny arms.
  No reference image needed (tier 1) — the bible in the prompt carries the design.

**The mouth is load-bearing.** Every speaking character's prompt names the mouth
("a visible animated mouth") and the dialogue block says the mouth animates naturally
with the words, loosely synced. Loose sync is the format's charm — never demand
frame-perfect lip-sync, never render a speaking character whose mouth is hidden.

## The appeal law (absolute)

Every character — villain, hero, or product — has **large expressive eyes and a warm,
readable face**. Emotive, lovable, alive — **NEVER uncanny, never dead-eyed, never
creepy, never a plastic doll**. The villain is pathetic and endearing, not scary; the
angry character is comically intense, not menacing. If a frame could read as uncanny or
gross, the design is wrong.

## Personality = mechanism (the casting law, from the primary bank)

Each character's PERSONALITY is its mechanism, acted through eyes, posture, and one
signature action. Cast every speaker by asking: *"if this mechanism were a person at a
party, who is it?"*

| Mechanism shape | Personality | Signature acting |
|---|---|---|
| The problem itself (villain) | defeated, sweaty, apologetic | slumps, droops, grieves WITH the viewer |
| Fights / reduces a threat | cocky brawler | snatches and punches the threat-prop |
| Blocks / processes / defends | armored knight | sword-slashes the drifting swarm |
| Gives energy / calm / time | wholesome innocent | sweet shrug, a gauge fills above its head |
| Burns / destroys a mass | barely-contained ember | glows hotter, the mass melts around it |
| The hero product | proud enforcer | arms crossed on a pedestal, label forward |

One personality per character, one character per mechanism — never compress two mechanisms
into one speaker, and never give two speakers the same personality.

## The character bible template (locked wording, filled per character)

Lock ONE bible block per character at Step 1 and paste it VERBATIM into every prompt that
character appears in (only the emotion/energy slot may move). Template:

> The character is a cute Pixar-style 3D animated [WHAT THE OBJECT IS — its real material,
> shape, and colors] with a face built into its own body: big expressive eyes [+ the
> personality's eye detail — heavy exhausted lids / confident thick brows / sharp V-brows],
> a visible animated mouth, and tiny [stubby/root/leaf] arms. [Posture and personality in
> one clause.] Charming and lovable, never creepy, never photoreal.

For a personified real product, open with the product identity instead: "The character is
the real [PRODUCT NAME] bottle/pack from the attached reference — real shape, label, text
and colors fully intact and readable — with big expressive eyes and a visible animated
mouth built into the label art, [muscular/tiny] arms, and short legs."

## The voice casting law (the voice lives in the prompt)

Seedance casts each character's voice from the prompt's description — there is no voice
upload, ever. Every dialogue block names the voice in 4–7 words that lock age, texture,
and energy: *"a small, hoarse, utterly exhausted deadpan voice"* · *"a warm, cocky,
confident voice"* · *"a low, smoldering, deliberate voice"* · *"a bright, kind, light
voice."* The voice description is part of the character's bible — identical wording every
time that character speaks. Because the voice is re-cast fresh on every generation, **a
character who speaks in two clips carries its FIRST clip as a video reference in the
second** (the reference ladder, tier 3) — that is the only way the same voice comes back.

## Prop grammar (every claim is a touchable object)

Nothing abstract is ever merely described — every mechanism/claim floats in the world as
a VISIBLE, TOUCHABLE object the character interacts with: threats as glowing orbs or
drifting molecule swarms (punched, slashed), progress as a live gauge that fills WHILE the
character talks, a mass problem (fat, clutter, debt) as the entire landscape the character
visibly shrinks. If a claim has no object, the beat isn't ready. One prop interaction per
scene, timed to the claim words.

## World grammar (one world per character, alternate the register)

Every speaking character gets its OWN world; no environment repeats anywhere in the ad.
Worlds alternate between **domestic real-space** (kitchen table, living room, counter —
"this belongs in your day") and **inside-the-body / inside-the-problem** (glistening
tunnels, fat landscapes, the split-open bicep — "this works where it counts"). Never let
two consecutive scenes share a world type. Body-interior worlds are warm, luminous, and
storybook-charming — never gross, never clinical, never horror. The CTA lands back in
daily life (a kitchen counter, a desk).

## Who gets a face (two proven modes — pick per brand at intake)

- **Full-cast mode (the primary bank's mode, default):** EVERYONE is personified —
  villain, ingredient heroes, and the hero product(s) (eyes and mouth built into the real
  label, label fully readable). Warm, comedic, family-feed friendly.
- **Dignity mode (the Alpha Serum mode):** only the villain / roasted old-ways get faces;
  the hero product stays a straight, un-personified product presented by the cast.
  Sharper, more premium.

State the chosen mode in the script header; it decides whether the product scenes need a
CASTING STILL (full-cast) or just the product still (dignity).

## The color grammar

The flagship's grade narrates by register, not by decline arc: the villain's world is
dimmer and cooler (blue-lit gym, the problem's gloom) with the character's material warm;
each hero's world is warm and vibrant (golden-hour kitchen, luminous body-interior); the
product reveal goes aspirational (dark steel + one warm toplight, chalk dust); the CTA is
the warmest frame of the ad (under-cabinet glow on a morning counter). Name the light in
every prompt; keep the whole ad inside the one warm, vibrant Studio grade.

## On-screen text: NONE — except the CTA's in-scene promo text (the one exception)

**No generation contains text** — no captions, no subtitles, no banner words, no invented
UI. The characters' own dialogue carries every claim; the source ads' burned-in word
captions are NOT reproduced (the family caption law stands — no caption overlay unless the
member explicitly asks, and the stitcher has no caption path).

**THE ONE EXCEPTION:** the **CTA clip only** may contain the offer as **in-scene,
diegetic 3D text** — a glowing dimensional object floating IN the scene (the flagship's
"70% OFF"), lit by the scene and casting light on it, a PROP not an overlay. It appears
in no other clip, ever. The only other readable text in any frame is the real product's
own label.

## The screen law (member hard rule, family-wide)

NEVER render a computer/phone screen CLOSE-UP without a reference image. A close, legible
screen appears ONLY in a scene that attaches the real screenshot. Everywhere else,
monitors and phones are ambient out-of-focus glow, never a readable close-up.

## Master negative prompt (attach to every scene — in the payload's `negative_prompt` FIELD)

No creepy uncanny face, no dead eyes, no uncanny valley, no horror styling, no gore, no
gross-out, no plastic doll look, no glossy plastic over render, no mixed art styles, no
claymation or clay texture, no photorealistic human, no live action, no real photograph,
no 2D illustration, no anime style, no flat cartoon, no extra limbs, no duplicated body
parts, no distorted anatomy, no warped or unreadable label, no melted or distorted
packaging, no out of frame subject, no on-screen text, no captions, no subtitles, no
words or letters, no watermark, no UI, no legible computer screen, no detailed screen
close-up, no fake user interface, no real people — no real human being or photoreal
person anywhere.

**Placement law:** the master negative lives in the payload's `negative_prompt` field
(and is handed to a Path A member as its own separate block) — it is NEVER embedded
inside the prompt text, where its banned-look tokens would read as requests.

(For the CTA clip — where the offer renders as in-scene 3D text — drop "no on-screen
text, no captions, no subtitles, no words or letters" from THAT scene's negative only;
keep "no watermark, no UI". For a scene that attaches a real screenshot, drop "no legible
computer screen, no detailed screen close-up"; keep "no fake user interface".)

## Good scene description formula

A strong talking-object scene names: (1) the speaking character with its bible VERBATIM,
(2) the world (this character's own, never reused), (3) the mechanism PROP and the one
signature interaction, (4) the timed action beats — the character stops, turns to the
lens, delivers the line, the prop pays off on the claim words, (5) **the emotion ("THE
EMOTION IS THE SUBJECT: ...")**, (6) the DIALOGUE block: the exact quoted line + the
locked voice casting + "mouth animating naturally with the words, loosely synced",
(7) the AUDIO line: only the character's voice + the world's soft ambience, no music,
no other voices, (8) the locked style block, (9) the master negative.

**Vague → strong:**
- "The enzyme is tired" → "The enzyme has stopped mid-push, boulder-sized food chunk
  rolling back past it; heavy eyelids at half-mast, tiny arms hanging, one slow exhausted
  blink — total weary resignation, not anger."
- "The product looks strong" → "The matte-black bottle stands arms-crossed on a stack of
  45 LB iron plates, chalk dust drifting through one warm toplight, its label eyes fixed
  on the lens, label text pin-sharp and fully readable."

## Final rule

Every talking-object scene should feel like a beloved animated character caught
mid-confession: charming enough to love, specific enough to sell, and readable in one
glance. The best output is appeal + a personality you can cast in one word + a claim you
can touch + a voice you can hear before it renders.
