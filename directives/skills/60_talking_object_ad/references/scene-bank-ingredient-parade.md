# Scene Bank — INGREDIENT PARADE (PRIMARY BANK)

**Rank: PRIMARY.** When multiple talking-object banks exist, FILL FROM THIS ONE FIRST.
The other banks (Alpha Serum self-roast, GOOGUT gut-world) are secondary color;
this is the flagship. It carries the most guidance — when a rule here conflicts
with a secondary bank, this bank wins.

**Source:** TestBoost Max + Burn Evolved stack ad (Sculpt Nation), 53.9s, 716x1280 @ 24fps.
**Captured:** source file had no audio; the script below was recovered word-for-word

## THE FORMAT AT A GLANCE

Seven scenes, 53.9s, and **every single word is spoken by a character on screen.**
There is no detached narrator voice anywhere in this ad (the one ambiguous segment,
T06, is discussed in its shell). That is the defining law of this format:

> **LAW 1 — THE CAST CARRIES THE AD.** Nobody talks ABOUT the product world;
> the world talks for itself. Villain, ingredients, and product all speak in
> first person, mouths animated, loosely synced. Render dialogue clips AUDIO ON
> with the line in the prompt; the voice and mouth come out of the generation together.

The arc:

```
T01  VILLAIN HOOK      the problem personified INSIDE the customer's body, self-roasts   5.5s
T02  INGREDIENT HERO 1 "Hi, I'm ___" — mechanism vs. floating prop, world 1              9.7s
T03  INGREDIENT HERO 2 same formula, NEW personality, NEW world                         10.0s
T04  INGREDIENT HERO 3 same formula, NEW personality, NEW world                          7.6s
T05  INGREDIENT HERO 4 same formula, NEW personality, NEW world                          7.7s
T06  PRODUCT DUO       the stack revealed as characters, powers combined                 8.3s
T07  CTA               products point at the link, in-scene 3D promo text                5.1s
```

Cut rhythm: 7 segments, average 7.7s, range 5.1–10.0s — lands on the family's
4–9s scene law almost exactly. Hook is the shortest beat before the CTA; the
mechanism scenes get the longest holds.

### The dialogue formula (LAW 2)

Every ingredient hero speaks the SAME sentence skeleton. This repetition is not
lazy — it is the format's rhythm, like a chorus. Bank it verbatim:

> **"Hi, I'm [INGREDIENT]. I may help [MECHANISM VERB] [THE BAD THING],
> so you can stop [PAIN] and start [DESIRE]. With diet and exercise."**

Three load-bearing pieces:

1. **"I may help"** — the FTC hedge is INSIDE the character's voice. It reads as
   humility ("I may help…") instead of legal boilerplate. Never write "I will,"
   "I fix," "I cure." The hedge is charming AND compliant. Non-negotiable for
   supplements; keep it for every claim category that regulators watch.
2. **"so you can stop ___ and start ___"** — pain and desire in one breath,
   always concrete and daily-life sized ("stop crashing at 3pm and actually have
   energy for your family"), never clinical.
3. **The compliance close ("with diet and exercise")** — attached to every
   body-composition claim (testosterone, estrogen, fat loss) but NOT to the pure
   energy claim (T04 has no close). The source is precise about this: hedge where
   the claim is regulated, breathe where it isn't. Copy that judgment.

### Personality = mechanism (LAW 3)

Each character's PERSONALITY is its mechanism, acted:

| Character | Mechanism | Personality | How it's acted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle Loss (villain) | decline | defeated, sweaty, apologetic | slumps, drips sweat, shares the viewer's loss |
| Ashwagandha | fights stress hormones | cocky brawler | snatches and punches red orbs mid-line |
| DIM | processes estrogen | armored knight | sword slashes through molecule clusters |
| enXtra | all-day energy | wholesome innocent | sweet shrug, battery fills above its head |
| Coleus | burns stored fat | angry ember | glows hotter, fat melts around it |
| TestBoost Max | strength | stoic enforcer | arms crossed, unmoving |
| Burn Evolved | fire/burn | barely-contained rage | flame arms, gritted teeth, conjures the stolen orb |

When filling: cast each ingredient/feature by asking "if this mechanism were a
person at a party, who is it?" — then let the eyes, posture, and one signature
action carry it. The emotion is the subject.

### Prop grammar (LAW 4)

**Every mechanism is a VISIBLE, TOUCHABLE object in the scene.** Nothing abstract
is ever merely described — it floats in the world and the character interacts with it:

- stress hormones → red glowing orbs with fiery trails (ashwagandha punches them)
- excess estrogen → pink molecule clusters (DIM's sword slashes them)
- sustained energy → holographic battery above the head, filling red→green in
  real time as the character talks (the progress bar is IN the shot)
- stored fat → glossy amber globules forming the entire landscape
- "the fat that's STEALING your energy" → a glowing golden orb held in the
  product's fist — an abstract verb turned into a stealable object

When filling: for each claim in the script, ask "what object is this?" and put
that object in the character's hands (or sword, or orbit). If a claim has no
object, the beat isn't ready.

### World grammar (LAW 5)

Seven scenes, seven worlds, ZERO repeats — and they alternate between
**domestic real-space** and **inside-the-body**:

split bicep (body) → kitchen table (home) → tissue tunnel (body) → living room
(home) → fat landscape (body) → gym (aspiration space) → kitchen counter (home, CTA)

The alternation is the escalation law applied to geography: every cut teleports
the viewer somewhere new, so nobody gets bored, and the body-interior shots keep
paying off the "this works INSIDE you" promise. Fill rule: never let two
consecutive scenes share a world type, and land the CTA back in daily life
(kitchen counter = "this belongs in your morning").

### Who gets a face (LAW 6 — updated by this bank)

In THIS flagship, **everyone is personified** — villain, ingredients, AND the two
hero products (eyes and mouth integrated into the label art, arms, legs). In the
Alpha Serum secondary bank, only the roasted old-ways get faces and the hero
stays a straight product. Both modes are proven. Pick per brand:

- **Full-cast mode (this bank):** warm, comedic, family-feed friendly. Product
  faces must be built INTO the label design (eyes sit in the label art, the real
  label text stays fully readable). Requires the real product still as reference
  — ladder tier 2, no exceptions.
- **Dignity mode (Alpha Serum bank):** the hero product never gets a face;
  competitors self-roast, hero arrives straight. Sharper, more premium.

### Reference ladder + the drift this bank proves (LAW 7)

- T01–T05 characters are generic one-off speakers → **no reference image**
  (ladder tier 1). Bible in the prompt is enough; each speaks once and is gone.
- T06–T07 products → **real product still required** (tier 2), because labels
  must read.
- T06 → T07 is the SAME two characters in a second scene → **T06's rendered clip
  is the video reference for T07** (tier 3, voice + face continuity), new
  environment per LAW 5.

**The source itself shows why tier 3 exists:** between the gym scene and the CTA,
the bottles' eyes visibly change style (label-integrated angry eyes → simple
rounded googly eyes). That drift is exactly what passing the first clip as video
reference prevents. Do not repeat the source's mistake.

### Text law (LAW 8)

The source ran burned-in word-by-word captions. **Our builds do not** — the
family caption law stands (VO/dialogue carries every word; no caption overlay
unless the member explicitly asks). The ONE text allowance, straight from this
source: **in-scene, diegetic 3D promo text in the CTA clip only** — the glowing
"70% OFF" floating IN the scene above the products, lit by the scene, casting
glow on the counter. It is a prop, not an overlay. Nowhere else, ever.

### T01 — VILLAIN HOOK: the problem lives in your body (0:00.0–0:05.5, 5.5s)

**Banked evidence.** A huge Pixar-style man mid-flex fills the frame edge; his
bicep is split open like a cutaway diagram, and sitting slumped INSIDE the muscle
is his MUSCLE LOSS — a limp, deflated strip of muscle fiber with drooping googly
eyes, tiny stick arms, and beads of sweat rolling off it. The man's face looms in
the top corner, looking down at it. Gym behind: cool blue fluorescents, racked
plates, warm skin tones against cold steel. Camera: static medium close-up on the
villain; the human is set dressing, not the subject.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"Hey, I'm your muscle loss. Remember when you used to be
strong? Yeah… me too."**

**Why it works.** Triple hook in 5.5s: (1) a body cut open = pattern interrupt,
(2) the villain introduces itself BY the viewer's own pain name — "I'm YOUR
muscle loss" is a callout without saying "hey you," (3) "Yeah… me too" — the
villain grieves WITH you. Self-roast plus shared loss beats any narrator opener.
The customer appears exactly once in the whole ad — here, as the landscape the
villain lives in, witnessing his own problem.

**Fill guidance.**
- `[VILLAIN]` = the customer's problem, named in the customer's own words (from
  VOC): muscle loss, thinning hair, 3pm crash, cluttered inbox. Not the
  competitor product (that's the Alpha Serum bank's hook) — the PROBLEM itself.
- Design law: the villain is made OF the problem's material (muscle loss = limp
  muscle fiber; brain fog = a damp grey cloud; debt = a crumpled bill) with
  googly eyes, tiny weak arms, sweat/droop. Pathetic, never scary.
- Put it INSIDE the customer's world-body if the product works internally;
  perch it ON the relevant object otherwise (on the keyboard, on the scalp).
- Line skeleton: `"Hey, I'm your [PROBLEM]. Remember when you [HAD THE GOOD
  THING]? Yeah… me too."` Keep the "Yeah… me too" beat — the shared grief IS
  the hook. 12–16 words, deadpan-sad delivery, mouth loosely synced.
- Hook beat: the villain's eyes meet the lens within the first 1–2 seconds.
- Render: AUDIO ON, voice = small, deflated, apologetic. No reference image
  (tier 1) unless a real product appears in frame.

### T02 — INGREDIENT HERO, THE BRAWLER (0:05.5–0:15.2, 9.7s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut: farmhouse kitchen, golden-hour light through the
window. A gnarled tan ASHWAGANDHA ROOT stands on the table like a tiny champion —
thick confident brows, smug grin, root-tendril legs planted wide. RED GLOWING
ORBS (stress hormones) with fiery comet trails orbit him. Mid-line he snatches
one out of the air and PUNCHES it — impact flash — timed to "so you can stop."
Ends open-armed: "yourself again." Camera: static full-body, table height,
character centered, shallow depth.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"Hi, I'm ashwagandha. I may help reduce stress hormones
that block your natural testosterone, so you can stop feeling drained and start
feeling like yourself again. With diet and exercise."**

**Why it works.** First hero enters 5.5s in — the ad flips from grief to swagger
in one cut. The mechanism ("reduces stress hormones") is never explained; it's
CHOREOGRAPHED — he literally punches a stress hormone on the pain word. The
domestic kitchen grounds the exotic ingredient in the viewer's morning.

**Fill guidance.**
- `[INGREDIENT]` = the actual ingredient/feature, rendered as itself with a face
  (the root IS the body — face built into its own form, per the app template).
- Personality: the fighter. Give the confidence to the character whose mechanism
  is DEFENSE/reduction.
- `[PROP]` = the enemy object (LAW 4), orbiting from frame one, destroyed once,
  ON the pain word. One punch, not a brawl — 9s holds one signature action.
- Full formula line with compliance close (regulated claim). 15–25 words max — note the
  source's own verbatim lines run over the pipeline's 25-word ceiling (T02 is 30 words,
  T03 is 33): the SOURCE's ad allowed 10s scenes, ours cap at 9s, so a fill always trims
  the line into the ceiling or splits the speaker across two scenes.
- World: domestic real-space #1. Kitchen = "part of your routine."
- Render: AUDIO ON, voice = confident, warm, a little cocky. Tier 1 (no ref).

### T03 — INGREDIENT HERO, THE KNIGHT (0:15.2–0:25.2, 10.0s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut INSIDE the body: a warm pink-orange tissue tunnel,
glowing rim light, floating particles. A BROCCOLI KNIGHT — broccoli crown as
helmet plume, muscular green stalk body in polished plate armor — stands guard
with a longsword. PINK MOLECULE CLUSTERS (estrogen) drift toward him; his sword
slashes through them on the claim words. Ends in a double-bicep flex on "and
exercise." Camera: full-body low hero angle, slight drift, molecules crossing
the foreground for depth.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"What's up, I'm DIM. I may help your body process excess
estrogen that makes you store fat like a woman, so you can build a more masculine
physique. With proper diet and exercise."**

**Why it works.** The greeting changes ("What's up" vs "Hi") — same formula,
different voice, so the chorus never goes stale. The armor/sword casting makes
"processes estrogen" FEEL like defense of the castle. Body-interior world pays
off "works inside you" right after a domestic scene (LAW 5 alternation).

**Fill guidance.**
- Personality: the guardian. Cast whichever ingredient/feature BLOCKS or
  processes a threat as armored, armed, disciplined.
- Vary the greeting every hero: Hi / What's up / Hey / (none, straight to "I'm").
- `[PROP]` = the threat as a drifting swarm; one clean sword-pass per claim word.
- The source's "store fat like a woman" is the brand's aggressive angle — swap
  freely; the SHELL only requires "the bad thing, made concrete."
- World: body-interior #2 — tunnel/vessel/tissue, warm and luminous, never gross.
- Render: AUDIO ON, voice = low, assured, knightly. Tier 1.

### T04 — INGREDIENT HERO, THE SWEETHEART (0:25.2–0:32.8, 7.6s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut: cozy living room, warm lamps, sofa bokeh. A small
cream-and-pink striped ROOT (enXtra) with huge round innocent eyes and a sweet
smile. Above its head floats a holographic BATTERY GAUGE that fills from
red/empty to bright green WHILE IT TALKS — the fill is synced to the lines
(empty on "Hi", half on "all day", full by "for your family"). Ends with an
open-armed shrug-presentation. Camera: static full-body, tabletop height.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"Hi, I'm enXtra. I may help give you sustained energy all
day, so you can stop crashing at 3pm and actually have energy for your family."**

**Why it works.** Personality flip #3: after brawler and knight, the CUTE one —
the range keeps the parade alive. The battery is the entire mechanism argument
with zero words spent on it. And the desire slot is the ad's most human moment:
not "performance," but "energy for your family." NO compliance close — the
energy claim doesn't need one, and the line breathes (LAW 2, point 3).

**Fill guidance.**
- Personality: the wholesome innocent. Give the cute casting to the mechanism
  whose OUTCOME is domestic/emotional (energy, sleep, calm, time saved).
- `[PROP]` = a live gauge (battery, meter, rising sun) that fills IN SYNC with
  the dialogue — this is the one shell where the prop's ANIMATION carries the
  progress-bar duty. Specify the sync in the prompt: "battery fills as it speaks,
  reaching full on the final word."
- Desire slot goes to the most human stake you have from VOC — family, evenings,
  patience. This is the parade's heart-beat scene; don't waste it on specs.
- World: domestic real-space #2, softer and warmer than T02's kitchen.
- Render: AUDIO ON, voice = bright, kind, light. Tier 1.

### T05 — INGREDIENT HERO, THE EMBER (0:32.8–0:40.5, 7.7s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut back inside the body: a landscape of glossy AMBER
FAT GLOBULES stretching to the horizon, warm backlight, heat shimmer. Planted
among them like an angry sapling: a single flame-red COLEUS LEAF with sharp
V-brows and an ember-orange core that glows through its veins. As it speaks, the
glow intensifies and the globules around it soften/melt; by the last line, small
GREEN SHOOTS have sprouted at its base — burn becomes life. Camera: static
medium, low angle, fat globules crossing the foreground.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"I'm Coleus Forskohlii. I may help your body burn stored
fat for energy, so that stubborn belly fat can finally start coming off. With
diet and exercise."**

**Why it works.** No greeting at all — fourth verse drops the "Hi" and hits
faster. The world IS the enemy (the fat isn't a prop he fights; he's standing in
it, outnumbered — and melting it anyway). The green shoots are the arc-in-a-shot:
destruction reframed as renewal. "Stubborn belly fat… finally" is pure VOC
language in the pain slot.

**Fill guidance.**
- Personality: the intense one. Cast the BURN/destroy mechanism as barely
  contained heat — anger pointed at the problem, never at the viewer.
- World-as-enemy variant of LAW 4: when the bad thing is a MASS (fat, clutter,
  debt), make it the entire landscape and let the character visibly shrink it.
- Include one renewal cue (green shoots, cleared desk corner, first ray of
  light) in the final second — escalation inside a single shot.
- Pain slot: the customer's own adjectives ("stubborn," "finally").
- Render: AUDIO ON, voice = low, smoldering, deliberate. Tier 1.

### T06 — PRODUCT DUO REVEAL: the stack assembles (0:40.5–0:48.8, 8.3s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut: a serious dark-steel gym, blue depth, one warm
yellow toplight, chalk dust in the air. On two stacks of 45 LB iron plates stand
the TWO PRODUCTS as characters: TESTBOOST MAX — matte black bottle, green label
type, determined angry eyes integrated INTO the label art, thick cream-colored
bodybuilder arms crossed — and BURN EVOLVED — matte black bottle, fiery red
label whose face is glowing eyes and gritted teeth, arms MADE OF FIRE, flexing.
Mid-scene Burn conjures and grips a GLOWING GOLDEN ORB — "the fat that's
stealing it" made stealable. Labels stay fully readable throughout. Camera:
slow push-in from low hero angle.

Dialogue (verbatim): **"Together with a steel and fire stack designed to help
you reclaim your masculine energy while torching the fat that's stealing it.
When combined with diet and exercise."**

**Attribution note (no audio in source):** the captions read as a bridge line.
Two valid voicings — (a) the duo speaks it in first-person plural ("Together,
we're a steel and fire stack…"), which keeps LAW 1's all-dialogue purity and
matches the CTA's "get us"; (b) a single announcer voice carries T06 only.
Default to (a). Whichever you pick, T07 must match it.

**Why it works.** The parade pays off: four mechanisms become ONE object pair the
viewer can buy. Element-naming ("steel and fire") gives the duo a combined
identity, and each bottle's design carries its element (crossed stone-still arms
vs. flame arms). The pedestal of 45 LB plates crowns them without a single spec
word. "Reclaim your masculine energy while torching the fat that's STEALING it"
— the enemy verb from the hook returns, now losing.

**Fill guidance.**
- One product? Same shell, solo pedestal, one element. Duo/stack? Give each an
  element pair with tension (steel/fire, calm/storm, day/night).
- Faces are built INTO the real label (eyes sit within the label art; every word
  of the label stays legible). **Real product still attached — tier 2, hard
  requirement.** The label is the face; the label must be true.
- Pedestal = an object from the customer's aspiration world (plates, cutting
  board, desk riser), never a podium.
- Prop: ONE conjured object that embodies the claim's verb (the stolen energy as
  a graspable orb).
- Render: AUDIO ON. Voice(s): deeper, unified, proud. **This clip becomes the
  video reference for T07** — flag it `voice_ref_source` in the manifest.

### T07 — CTA: point at the link (0:48.8–0:53.9, 5.1s)

**Banked evidence.** Hard cut: the same two bottle characters, now on a white
marble kitchen counter under warm under-cabinet light — the aspiration gym
traded for the viewer's own morning. Above them floats a giant glossy 3D
**"70% OFF"** in glowing white, lit BY the scene, casting soft glow on the
counter (diegetic prop, not an overlay — LAW 8). The bottles flex, then BOTH
POINT DOWN at where the platform's link/button lives. (Source drift note: the
bottles' eye style visibly changed from T06's label-integrated eyes to simpler
googly eyes — the inconsistency tier 3 exists to prevent. Use T06's clip as
video reference; don't inherit the mistake.)

Dialogue (verbatim): **"If you want to get us for 70% off, you can just click
the link below."**

**Why it works.** The product asks for the sale ITSELF — "get us" is friendlier
than any narrator's "buy now," and physically pointing at the link is the
platform-native gesture the whole format has been building toward. Five seconds,
one offer, one gesture, done.

**Fill guidance.**
- World: the customer's daily-life surface (kitchen counter, desk, bathroom
  shelf) — the CTA answers "where does this live in MY day?"
- Line skeleton: `"If you want to get us [OFFER], you can just click the link
  below."` First-person plural (or singular for one product). Sixteen words or
  fewer — the source's is exactly 16; the offer token counts.
- The pointing-down gesture is mandatory; time it to "link below."
- `[OFFER TEXT]` as in-scene 3D glowing text, CTA clip ONLY. No other text
  anywhere in the ad, ever.
- Render: AUDIO ON, **video reference = T06's rendered clip** (tier 3 — same
  speakers, voice + face continuity), environment changed per LAW 5, product
  still ALSO attached (labels must stay true). Sequential dependency: T07
  renders only after T06 is approved.

## FILLING THIS BANK — the short version

1. Villain = the customer's problem in their own words, living in/on their body
   or world. Self-roast + "Yeah… me too."
2. Pick 2–4 ingredients/features (the source used 4; fewer is fine — cut heroes,
   never compress two mechanisms into one character).
3. Cast each by personality (brawler / knight / sweetheart / ember — vary the
   greeting, vary the world, alternate domestic ↔ inside-the-body).
4. Every claim gets a touchable prop; every regulated claim gets "I may help"
   plus the diet-and-exercise close.
5. Product(s) revealed as personified characters on a pedestal from the
   aspiration world — real still attached, label readable, faces in the label.
6. CTA on a daily-life surface, product points at the link, offer as in-scene 3D
   text, T06's clip as video reference.
