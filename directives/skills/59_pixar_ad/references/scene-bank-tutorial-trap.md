## The systems that make it work

- **Two-act prop system.** The villain is a teetering tower of five mismatched pastel
  gadget boxes wired together and wrapped in silver duct tape — the DIY stack made
  physical, one glowing dot per box, no writing on any of them. The hero is ONE clean
  laptop showing the real dashboard. The silver tape roll is the arc in a single object:
  stretched proudly in the hook, dropped in the bin at the end.
- **The build IS the hook.** Act one plays the fantasy straight — sleeves-up optimism,
  cable clicks, dots flicking on — so the collapse lands as comedy, not lecture.
- **Deadpan physical comedy carries the sting.** The tower folds like flat-pack
  furniture in one continuous buckle while she watches with a flat unblinking stare,
  shoulders dropping. One laugh per act, each also carrying information.
- **Palette narrates.** Cozy warm build → one alarm-red dot → cold 3am monitor blue →
  morning gold at the turn → golden product end-card. The color arc is the emotional arc.
- **The real-dashboard law.** Screens stay out-of-focus ambient glow until the turn;
  the dashboard appears ONLY in scenes that attach the approved product still, and it is
  legible there. Monitor code-texture in the burnout scene must verify illegible.
- **The person-free CTA.** The shipped ending contains no character anywhere — a river
  of glowing blank gold cards arcs INTO the dashboard (the screen visibly fills as the
  VO says the batch is live), then a hard cut to the clean product push-in. Person-free
  endings are contamination-proof: Seedance cannot lip-sync a person who is not there.
- **Whisper adjudication.** Every clip is transcribed twice (plain + word_timestamps).
  Text that mutates between passes, degenerate alignments, or lone 100-200ms words over
  no-face windows are hallucinations; a coherent stable sentence over a visible mouth is
  real contamination and re-renders with a hardened silent-ending block (see T06).
