# Natural Voice — write it the way people actually talk (NO AI slop)

The fastest way to kill a Pixar/claymation/UGC ad is "AI slop": tidy, polished, written-not-spoken
copy. The voiceover has to sound like a real person talking to a friend — never like an ad
reading itself out loud. This is non-negotiable craft.

## HARD RULE: flowing spoken sentences, NOT clipped ad-fragments

Real people connect their thoughts with little words — "you just," "and it," "so then,"
"in the morning," "kind of." Ad copy strips those out into telegraphic stacks that nobody
says out loud. Write the connective tissue back in: every line should sound like one
continuous spoken thought, not a bullet point read aloud.

- ❌ "One scoop in water, first thing — covers your bases, not the whole shelf."
- ✅ "You just mix one scoop into water in the morning, and it kind of does what your whole
  cabinet was trying to."
- ❌ "Lighter. Less bloated. More energy."
- ✅ "A couple weeks in, your gut feels better, you're less bloated, and you've actually got
  energy that lasts."

The ONLY exception is a single deliberate punchline fragment for rhythm ("They smell his
presence."), used rarely and on purpose — never to compress an instruction or a benefit.

## The gold standard (study this)

> Did you know smell is the most powerful sense? A unique scent can make you unforgettable,
> but it doesn't mean you gotta drop $500 on one cologne. That's how that one bro, who looks
> mid, somehow gets all the girl's attention in restaurants, on the street, and even in
> groceries. They smell his presence. But perfume houses charge you over $350 for alcohol
> sprays that cost $10 to make. Penrose took the exact same scent molecules in expensive
> perfumes, like Baccarat 540, and mixed them with premium body lotion. Preserving the same
> scent as the original and making it last WAY longer while hydrating your skin. If you want
> it simple, get Penrose and smell irresistible for a fraction of the price with their
> 30-day guarantee.

## Why it works — the techniques (USE these)

1. **Spoken words, contractions, slang.** "gotta," "looks mid," "that one bro," "drop $500."
   Say it how people say it, not how they'd write it.
2. **A relatable archetype + a tiny story.** "that one bro who looks mid somehow gets all the
   girl's attention" — gossipy and specific, like telling a friend about someone you both know.
3. **Concrete everyday specifics.** "in restaurants, on the street, even in groceries." Real,
   named places, tossed off casually. Specifics beat abstractions every time.
4. **One deliberate punchline fragment for rhythm** — a COMPLETE little thought said for
   effect ("They smell his presence."), used rarely and on purpose. This is NOT the same as
   telegraphic ad-stacks (see the hard rule above): never compress an instruction or a
   benefit into a clipped comma-list.
5. **Talk TO one person.** Casual second person: "you gotta," "if you want it simple."
6. **Spoken emphasis.** A CAPS word for the stress you'd hear out loud ("last WAY longer").
7. **Casual numbers and name-drops.** "$350," "$10 to make," "Baccarat 540" — specific and a
   little insider/cheeky.
8. **A cheeky, slightly contrarian aside.** "alcohol sprays that cost $10 to make."
9. **Plain mechanism, no buzzwords.** Explain the how simply ("mixed them with premium body
   lotion").
10. **Imperfect, run-on spoken flow.** Thoughts connect the way speech does ("but it doesn't
    mean you gotta…", "That's how that one bro…").
11. **Voice the customer's own objection in their words** ("and before you go 'it's just a
    fancy multivitamin'…") — instantly real, and it disarms the skeptic.

## AI-slop tells (BAN these)

- "Imagine…" / "Picture…" / "What if…" openers.
- Parallel, listy structure: "The probiotics… The adaptogens…", "Not only… but also…".
- Clipped, telegraphic comma-stacks that drop the words speech uses: "One scoop in water,
  first thing." / "Lighter, less bloated, more energy." Keep the connective tissue ("you
  just…", "and it…", "in the morning").
- Writerly phrases nobody says out loud: "quietly sure," "trade all of it for one decision,"
  "the one thing that matters," "a testament to," "in a world where," "say goodbye to,"
  "look no further."
- Em-dashes used for literary rhythm; perfectly balanced clauses; zero contractions.
- Abstract benefit-speak instead of a concrete picture ("supports your wellness journey").
- Corporate words: elevate, optimize, unlock, premium experience, foundational, transform,
  seamless, effortless, game-changer, revolutionize.
- Hype punctuation: exclamation marks, emoji.

## The test

Read every line OUT LOUD. If you'd never say it to a friend at a kitchen table — if it
sounds like an announcer or a brochure — rewrite it. Match the brand's register (a frat-bro
product and a premium wellness brand sound like different people), but BOTH must sound like
a real human, just a different real human. Casual ≠ sloppy: it's still tight, specific, and
ends on the CTA.
