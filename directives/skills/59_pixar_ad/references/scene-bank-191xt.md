## The systems that make it work

- **One hero, and the FACE is the dashboard.** The recurring Pixar hero's expression,
  posture, frame, and the world's grade visibly change month over month — drained and
  dull at Week 1, steadying at Month 3, bright and full at Month 6. The transformation is
  never told, it is SHOWN (the source showed it as growing translucent muscle; the Pixar
  port shows it as posture, energy, a filling-out frame, and a warming grade).
- **The world is one cohesive animated world, and nobody blinks.** Pixar-style gyms,
  bedrooms, a clinic; Pixar-style witnesses (a partner, two gym peers, a nurse) treat the
  hero as a completely normal person — the deadpan conceit.
- **The time-milestone spine.** Week 1 → Week 3 → Month 2 → 3 → 5 → 6. Each milestone is a
  new scene, a new location, and a new emotion state. The VOICEOVER carries every
  milestone ("Week three: ...") and the footage stays clean.
- **The witness ladder.** Proof escalates through OTHER people: his body → his shirts → his
  partner (leaning on his shoulder before he says a word) → gym peers asking questions → a
  nurse and a doctor with normal results → finally his own mirror. Each act hands the proof
  to a more objective witness.
- **The product is real-labeled, early, and recurring.** The Pixar-prop bottle (real label
  fully readable, from the approved product still) appears at second 3 on a gym bench with
  the world blurred behind it, returns as a macro in the mechanism act, and closes on a
  studio end-card.
- **Mechanism happens ON the body.** The invisible claim gets a visible animated effect:
  luminous energy rings orbiting the flexed arms (animation-native). The old belief dying =
  the whole gym DRAINING TO DESATURATED GREY while he alone stays in full color.
- **The forbidden-authority act.** "The industry calls this impossible" dramatized as a grey
  still-life of generic bottles, then the hero product staged like contraband. COMPLIANCE:
  banned-substance and regulator-name claims are a legal-risk slot — keep the
  establishment-vs-outsider MECHANIC, swap in claims the member's brand can lawfully make.
- **Split-screen CTA grammar.** The close runs action on top, product card on the bottom —
  two separate generations composited in the EDIT, never one generation.
- **Third-person case study.** The VO narrates "a man / he", documentary-cold; the character
  never talks, never lip-syncs. The voice is the external VO.
