# Pixar Scripting — DR-framework transcripts staged in a Pixar world

The Pixar ad is scripted EXACTLY like the claymation ad: a direct-response voiceover
built on ONE framework from the framework library, written the way people actually talk,
30–45 seconds, ending on the CTA. What makes it a PIXAR ad is the staging (Step 4+): the
beats are later rendered as a cast Pixar hero feeling the story inside a fully animated
world. This doc is the Step 1–2 craft: how to pick the framework and write the transcript.

**The two documents that govern the words:**
- `scripting-frameworks.md` — the 12-framework DR library (C.R.O.W.D.,
  D.I.S.R.U.P.T., C.U.R.E., F.O.U.N.D.E.R., S.I.M.P.L.E., P.U.R.E., PAS, U.G.L.Y.,
  P.R.O.V.E., S.H.O.W., Triple G, TEASE). Step 1 picks ONE per ad.
- `natural-voice.md` — the spoken-voice bar and the AI-slop banlist. Every
  line passes the read-it-out-loud test.

## Step 1 craft: pick the framework (never default to one shape)

Mine the VOC and Brand DNA first — the pain, the verbatim customer language, the
objections, the beliefs the buyer must hold before purchase. Then pick the ONE framework
whose mechanism installs the belief this ad targets, and say which and why in one line:

- Skeptical, burned-by-the-category audience → **D.I.S.R.U.P.T.** or **P.R.O.V.E.**
- A hated alternative with a visible contrast → **S.H.O.W. (Us vs. Them)** or **PAS**.
- A fear the product architecturally answers → **PAS wrapped around the fear, resolved
  by mechanism** (fear-to-control).
- An identity story ("she built the duct-tape stack") → **origin-scenario case study**
  told through S.I.M.P.L.E., Triple G, or P.U.R.E.
- A compounding-over-time benefit that genuinely wants a ladder → **TEASE (curiosity
  loop)** with a progression spine — the ONLY case where the "Day 1… Day 30…" shape is
  the right pick, and Bank 01 (191xt) is its staging reference.

Vary the framework ACROSS ads for the same brand: a three-way Meta test wants three
different frameworks, three different heroes, three different worlds — never the same
shape three times.

When a Foundation Pack exists, it decides WHAT to say (the avatar, the big idea, the
named mechanisms, which of the 6 purchase beliefs this ad installs); the VOC document
stays the source of verbatim customer language, and the transcript carries at least one
verbatim VOC phrase or the product's named mechanism.

## The narrator voice (two legal modes, never first person)

- **Third person — the case study (default).** "He asked AI for one ad, and it buried
  him alive." A narrator tells one person's story; the hero is the viewer's surrogate.
- **Second person — direct address.** "You've heard the story." Best for fear/contrast
  frameworks where the narrator talks straight at the buyer.

Pick ONE and hold it; the CTA line may address "you" directly in either mode. Never
first person.

## Beat-writing rules (the transcript IS the claymation rulebook)

- **Natural spoken voice, per `natural-voice.md`:** contractions, connective tissue,
  concrete everyday specifics, one deliberate punchline fragment at most, zero AI-slop
  tells, zero corporate words. Read every line out loud before keeping it.
- **DR cadence from the chosen framework** — the framework's beats become the line
  roles (hook → agitate/problem → tease/turn → mechanism → proof/payoff → CTA). The
  hook line lands its first beat inside 1–2 seconds; the ad ENDS on the CTA line.
- **One idea per line.** Each line will become one scene's staging; two ideas crammed
  into one line means a muddy scene later.
- **Concrete over abstract — visceral beats (the Pixar staging asset):** translate every
  claim into something a Pixar character can physically FEEL or a witness can react to.
  This is what Step 4 stages emotion-first:

| Abstract claim | Sayable, stageable beat |
|---|---|
| Generic AI output is bad | "Every hook sounded the same, and the customer language felt fake." |
| Risky to automate | "Hand an AI the keys while you sleep." |
| DIY stacks break | "One tool updated, and the whole thing folded like IKEA furniture." |
| It's a lot of work | "Three a.m., she's debugging context windows instead of running ads." |
| Human oversight | "AI can analyze, but humans decide." |

- **Open a loop when the framework wants one** (TEASE, D.I.S.R.U.P.T.'s secret,
  P.R.O.V.E.'s doubt) and pay it off before the CTA; stack a small unresolved tease
  between acts so each line pulls into the next.
- **Earn the product — the TURN.** Build the tension first, then pivot on ONE clean
  hinge line where the product enters as the answer. The turn is a physical ARRIVAL in
  the staging, never an ad break.
- **The witness principle (staging, not words):** proof escalates through reactions —
  but on screen every witness is a PIXAR-STYLE character, never a real human. The VO may
  name real-world witnesses; the world renders them animated.

## Length law (identical to the clay skill)

**The finished voiceover must run 30 to 45 seconds** — a natural narrated read is only
~2.3–2.7 words per second, so that is roughly **75–110 words, 5–8 short lines**. Err
short. The whisper step reports the REAL length; over 45s means cut a line and re-voice.
Compress by CUTTING beats, never by cramming the framework's full arc into less air.

## The house quality bar (in-house, shipped)

- **D.I.S.R.U.P.T., third person, belief "AI output is generic slop":** "He asked AI
  for one ad, and it buried him alive in generic AI slop… Then he figured it out: once
  you treat it like a system, not 'make me an ad,' it actually works."
- **PAS fear-to-control, second person, belief "automation gets accounts banned":** "The
  fastest way to lose your ad account? Hand an AI the keys while you sleep… il sistema
  runs on one rule: AI can analyze, but humans decide."
- **Origin-scenario + limit-overload case study, third person (Bank 02, tutorial-trap):**
  "The tutorial said: Claude, plus four tools, equals ten grand a month. She believed
  it… Then one tool updated, and the whole thing folded like IKEA furniture."

Each pairs a framework with a belief and a distinct hero — that variety is the bar.

## The progression option (legal, no longer the default)

The "What happens if you ___? Day 1… Day 30…" escalating-progression shape remains ONE
legal framework choice (TEASE + a time/quantity/stage ladder) for products whose benefit
genuinely compounds — and when picked, its spine markers are SPOKEN by the VO ("Day
fifteen: …"), never rendered as on-screen text, and Bank 01 (`scene-bank-191xt.md`) is
the staging reference. Diagnose first; never reach for the ladder out of habit.
