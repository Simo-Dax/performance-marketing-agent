# Hook anatomy, the three ingredients and the first-frame gate

This shared module is the plugin's single definition of what a video hook IS and how much of the effort each part deserves. Load it in every video skill before the hook is written, and read it together with the hook-mechanic library in `headline-frameworks.md` H.4 (which owns WHAT the line says; this file owns the anatomy and the gates).

## HA.1 The three ingredients and their default weights

A video hook is three things arriving at once, and they are nowhere near equal:

- **The COPY, about 80 percent of the weight.** The written opening line is the hook. It gets the candidate treatment (HA.2), the punch check (`director-review.md` D.2b), and the clarity law (`creative-constraints.md` C.8). A weak line is never rescued by a strong visual.
- **The FIRST FRAME (the scroll-stopper), about 15 percent.** The visual floor the copy stands on. It has one job: interrupt the scroll long enough for the line to land.
- **The AUDIO, about 5 percent.** Voice tone, music, or silence. The smallest needle mover; make a sane default choice and spend the saved attention on the copy.

The weights are for effort and judgment, not a scoring formula. One skill runs a modified anatomy: `animate-static` has no audio ingredient, so its weights fall on the concept line and the MOTION.

## HA.2 The candidate rule (text is cheap, renders are not)

Hook copy is never written once and kept. Draft AT LEAST 3 genuinely different written candidates per final hook, pulling different mechanics from `headline-frameworks.md` H.4, then kill down through the clarity and punch gates to the one that survives. Dedicated banks go far wider.

This multiplication is TEXT ONLY, and that is a hard boundary: rendered hook assets (reference stills, hook reels, opening clips) stay at exactly ONE per ad unless the user explicitly orders more. Nothing in this module ever multiplies anything that costs credits or usage.

## HA.3 The first-frame gate ("what did I just watch")

The floor test for the visual ingredient: would a stranger, shown ONLY the first second, stop and need to know what they are looking at? If the first frame could open any ad in the category, it fails the floor.

When it runs, and this order is binding:

1. **Before rendering, on the hook prompt or plan.** Free, always, in every skill. This is where failures are supposed to be caught.
2. **At most once on the rendered hook asset, at the skill's EXISTING user checkpoint** (the reference-still approval in the voiceover factories, hook reel A in the fan-out factory). It is a named criterion the user is already answering at that gate, not a new gate and not a re-render trigger. A rendered hook that fails is the user's call at that checkpoint: adjust, re-render, or accept, governed entirely by the skill's own spend gates.

Never a loop. This gate has no authority to render anything, and it never runs twice on the same rendered asset.

What the gate judges, per skill family:

- **Generated hook stills** (clay, skeleton, pixar, vox, and the UGC formats): the still itself.
- **User-supplied likeness** (podcast): the opening LINE and the framing choice only. The gate never judges the user's own face, their chosen host, or an uploaded photo.
- **animate-static**: the MOTION concept, what happens in the first second of movement. The frame itself is the user's approved static and is never re-judged.

## HA.4 Floor, not promotion

HA.3 is a floor under the visual 15 percent, not a promotion of it. The copy still carries the hook: the punch and clarity gates run on the line FIRST, and an arresting visual with a weak line fails the hook while a strong line over a merely competent visual can pass. When effort is short, it goes to the words.

## HA.5 The spend law (binding)

Nothing in this module spends. Hook candidates are text. The rendered-asset check rides a checkpoint that already exists. The consuming skill's own spend gates (GATE 1, GATE 2, the path confirmations) remain the only authority for anything that costs credits or usage, and no instruction in this file may be read as permission to generate.
