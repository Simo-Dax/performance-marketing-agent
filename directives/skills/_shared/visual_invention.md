# Visual invention, the balance between contrast and renderability

This shared module governs HOW a storyboard turns a spoken line into a picture. Load it in every animated ad skill before writing scenes. It exists because the two ways a video ad dies are opposites, and avoiding one usually causes the other:

- **Boring.** It looks like an ad. Studio floor, product on a pedestal, character smiling at camera, the same framing every brand uses. The viewer has seen it ten thousand times, recognizes it as advertising in half a second, and scrolls.
- **Broken.** It was so ambitious the model could not render it. Crowds, complex physics, six things interacting, tiny mechanical detail. What comes back is smeared, warped, or nonsense, and that reads as cheap.

The way through is not a compromise between them. It is knowing which axes are free and which are constrained, then being aggressive on the free ones and disciplined on the rest.

## VI.1 The balance law

**Be radical in WHAT you show. Be simple in HOW MUCH is moving.**

Contrast comes from the CHOICE, never from the clutter. An unexpected place, an unexpected scale, an unexpected material, an unexpected camera position: all of these are free, because the model renders one clear subject just as easily in a strange world as a dull one. What costs reliability is the NUMBER of independent moving parts in frame, not how imaginative the frame is.

One bold idea per shot, rendered plainly, beats five ordinary ideas fighting each other.

## VI.2 The free axes, push hard here

These carry the contrast, and cost nothing in render reliability:

- **Environment.** The single highest-leverage axis. A line about being tired does not need a bedroom. Put it somewhere nobody would: an empty stadium, a laundromat at 3am, inside a fridge, a parking garage, a field, a stairwell. Change the world between scenes rather than crowding one world.
- **Scale.** Shrink the character against something enormous, or make an everyday object tower over them. Scale reads instantly at thumbnail size.
- **Material and texture.** What the world is made of is a free variable inside the skill's own style law.
- **Camera position.** Extreme low, extreme high, from inside something, through a gap, pressed close. A strange angle on a simple subject is cheap to render and impossible to ignore.
- **Metaphor made physical.** The invisible thing gets a body: the problem is an object that follows the character, the benefit is a color that spreads. Physical metaphors render reliably because they are just objects.
- **Composition against type.** Traditional ads center the product and light it evenly. Do the opposite when the line allows.

## VI.3 The constrained axis, stay disciplined here

Complexity of ACTION is where renders fail. In every shot:

- **One clear subject doing ONE clear action.** Two simultaneous actions is the ceiling, and only when one is background.
- **Few independent moving parts.** Every additional thing moving on its own multiplies failure.

Reliably bad, avoid unless the skill's own bank proves otherwise: crowds and groups doing different things; precise mechanical motion (working machinery, gears, assembly); liquid physics beyond a simple pour; hands doing fine manipulation; anything requiring exact object counts; readable text that is not explicitly allowed by the skill; fast complex camera moves stacked on top of fast subject motion.

## VI.4 The traditional-ad test

Before a scene is accepted, ask: **would a normal brand shoot this shot?** If yes, change one free axis until the answer is no. This is not weirdness for its own sake; the scene still has to serve the line it carries. A scene that is strange AND says nothing is worse than a plain one.

## VI.5 The two failure checks, both directions

Every scene passes both, or it is rewritten:

1. **The boring check.** Could this shot appear in any competitor's ad with the product swapped? If yes, it has no contrast. Move it on a free axis.
2. **The renderability check.** Count the independent moving parts and name the single action. If you cannot state the action in one short sentence, the model cannot render it. Simplify the ACTION, never the idea.

The target every time: a stranger cannot place what they are looking at, and the model renders it cleanly on the first attempt.

## VI.6 Story first, always

Invention serves the line. Every scene still carries the words it covers at that moment, and the arc still reads in order. A brilliant image attached to the wrong beat is a failed scene. When a free axis fights the meaning of the line, the line wins and a different axis moves instead.
